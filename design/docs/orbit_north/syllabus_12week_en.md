# ORBIT NORTH

**Space Mission Design and Operations — 12-week format — 3 credits**
GNG 3100 — Topics in Engineering I  ·  SYS 5186 — Advanced Topics in Systems Engineering  ·  Winter 2027 (draft)

> **Draft.** This is the 12-week version of the ORBIT NORTH summer intensive:
> the same outcomes, material and assessments, paced across a regular term.

## Course Information

**Format.** 3-credit course over a **12-week term**, delivered in person at
the University of Ottawa. Each regular week has two sessions:

| Session | Length | What happens | Location |
|---------|--------|--------------|----------|
| A — Lecture and seminar | 1.5 h | Short lecture on the week's topic, discussion, guest speakers | Classroom, TBC |
| B — Studio | 3 h | Team work in the Concurrent Design Facility (Weeks 1–7) or the mission control room (Weeks 8–12) | SpaceCDF lab · uOttawa Cyberrange |

The term has four blocks: **introduction** (Week 1 and the first half of
Week 2), **concurrent design** (from the Week 2 studio to the PDR at the end
of Week 7), **operations** (two weeks of training, Weeks 8–9, then two
simulation weeks, Weeks 10–11) and a **review** of the whole mission in
Week 12. The two **simulation weeks (Weeks 10 and 11)** replace the studio
with one **extended 6-hour block** at the Cyberrange, because a simulated
operations shift cannot be split. A one-week **reading break** falls after
Week 6.

**Mode.** In person. The CDF and simulation components require physical
attendance; recordings of the short lectures, the course videos and the
open textbook are available online.

## Official Course Description

ORBIT NORTH is a space systems course delivered by the University of
Ottawa School of Engineering Design and Teaching Innovation (SEDTI).
Students move from the Canadian space ecosystem and its regulatory
environment, through a complete preliminary CubeSat mission design
produced in a Concurrent Design Facility, to the operator seat in a live
mission simulation. The course is designed as a primer for professional
practice in the Canadian and international space sector for students with
no prior space background.

## Additional Course Description

The term follows the same arc as the summer intensive, in four blocks.

**Part 1 — Introduction (Week 1 and the first half of Week 2).** The
Canadian space ecosystem, the systems-engineering process, the concurrent
design method and the SpaceCDF platform; from a mission need to a problem
statement, stakeholders and measures of effectiveness.

**Part 2 — Concurrent design (Week 2 studio to Week 7).** The Week 2 studio
opens the CDF: the mission alternatives, concept v0 and a first concept of
operations, presented at an informal Mission Concept Review (MCR) at the end
of Week 2. Week 3 closes the MCR actions, locks the concept and covers the
Canadian regulatory landscape — the Remote Sensing Space Systems Act,
spectrum licensing through ISED, export controls, debris mitigation and
registration — for the team's regulatory brief. Then one CDF week per design
stage: System-V and the requirements baseline (Week 4; PRR and SRR are
explicitly collapsed into this week); orbit, power, AOCS and thermal
(Week 5); comms, structure and propulsion with two formal trade studies
(Week 6); and integration, V&V, cost and risk closure, ending with the
PDR-style review (Preliminary Design Review), in Week 7. The weekly rhythm
leaves time between CDF sessions for each discipline lead to do the analysis
that the intensive compresses into evenings.

The systems-engineering frameworks themselves — System-V, requirements
engineering, functional decomposition, margin policy, trade studies,
risk, and planning — are learned **inside** the CDF, because the
SpaceCDF platform embeds the SE process scaffolding, the risk
register, the planning baseline, and a regulatory-check module as
live tools.

**Part 3 — Operations (Weeks 8–11).** Two weeks of operations training at
the uOttawa Cyberrange: the ground segment, the mission control system and
the mission planner (Week 8), then the control room, the voice loop and dry
runs of every kind of pass (Week 9). Then two simulation weeks flying
EOSAT-1: LEOP and commissioning (Week 10), then nominal operations and
contingency (Week 11). In each simulation one team operates while another
observes and documents; the teams then swap.

**Part 4 — Review (Week 12).** A review of the whole mission, from the need
to the last pass: what the design decided, what operations showed about
those decisions, the structured debrief and a cohort-internal final mission
review.

There are no required textbooks to purchase. All readings, tools, and
software access — including SpaceCDF and the open textbook *Concurrent
Spacecraft Design* — are provided through Brightspace and the uOttawa
Cyberrange environment.

## Course Learning Outcomes

Learning outcomes are differentiated between the undergraduate (GNG 3100)
and graduate (SYS 5186) codes. All students pursue the shared outcomes;
graduate students additionally demonstrate the graduate-level outcomes
across the same activities.

### Shared outcomes — both codes

- **LO 1.** Describe the Canadian space ecosystem (past, present, emerging) and
  situate a given mission concept within it.
- **LO 2.** Apply the systems-engineering scaffolding embedded in a Concurrent
  Design Facility to structure a space mission concept through scope,
  ConOps, functional architecture, and preliminary budgets, drawing
  on ECSS and the NASA Systems Engineering Handbook.
- **LO 3.** Navigate Canadian satellite regulatory obligations — the Remote
  Sensing Space Systems Act, radiocommunication licensing, export
  controls, and debris mitigation — at a level sufficient to identify
  when professional advice is required.
- **LO 4.** Contribute substantively to a Concurrent Design Facility intensive
  design activity, taking a discipline role and delivering a complete
  preliminary CubeSat mission design across all principal subsystems.
- **LO 5.** Maintain a risk register and a planning baseline inside the CDF tool
  through the full design week.
- **LO 6.** Execute spacecraft operations procedures in a mission control
  environment, including nominal operations, planning, and anomaly
  response.
- **LO 7.** Collaborate in interdisciplinary student teams; give and receive
  structured technical critique.
- **LO 8.** Reflect critically on your own learning trajectory and on the
  ethical dimensions of space activity.

### Additional graduate outcomes — SYS 5186 only

- **LO G1.** Critically evaluate architecture trade-offs using quantitative
  methods (margin-based budgets, risk-adjusted comparison) and
  defend the evaluation in writing and in a review setting.
- **LO G2.** Lead a sub-area of the team's mission design and operations,
  coordinating contributions from other team members and managing
  interface definition.
- **LO G3.** Synthesise lessons learned from the simulation into a structured
  improvement proposal grounded in the systems engineering and
  human-factors literature.
- **LO G4.** Situate your experience in the course within at least one strand of
  the scholarly literature on space systems engineering, operations,
  or new-space practice.

## Teaching Philosophy

My role on this course is to accompany you into a professional
community. I bring standards, tools, and a network of colleagues — you
bring judgement, curiosity, and the willingness to make decisions
under uncertainty. The most important thing we will do together is
practise thinking about missions as whole systems, and practise making
decisions when the engineering is only part of what matters. You will
be asked to do things you don't yet feel qualified to do. That is not
an accident; it is the core of the course. Many space projects involve
doing something for the first time.

## Teaching Strategies and Approaches

- Short, concentrated lectures (usually ≤ 45 minutes) anchored to open
  NASA / ECSS material and the open textbook, used to set up the week's
  studio. Short course videos with practice questions accompany each
  topic.
- A six-week Concurrent Design Facility thread (Weeks 2–7) in which each student takes
  a discipline role and the team converges on a complete preliminary
  mission design, supported by the SpaceCDF platform's embedded SE
  process, risk, planning, and regulatory-check modules.
- Structured space-industry guest interactions throughout.
- A live, team-based mission simulation at the uOttawa Cyberrange, with
  alternating operator and observer roles.
- Peer-critique sessions structured around real review gates (Mission
  Concept Review, Preliminary Design Review).
- Individual reflective writing throughout the course.

## A note on review-gate terminology

The course uses NASA terminology: the *Mission Concept Review (MCR)* is
the end-of-Pre-Phase-A gate, and the *Preliminary Design Review (PDR)*
is the end-of-Phase-B gate. ECSS reuses the abbreviation "MCR" for the
Mission Close-out Review at the end of Phase E; we do not use that
meaning here. The Phase A intermediate reviews (Preliminary
Requirements Review, System Requirements Review) are explicitly
**collapsed into the first CDF design week (Week 4)** in this course; their content is delivered
inside the SpaceCDF requirements module.

## Time Commitment

A 3-credit course carries the same total workload whether it runs as an
intensive or across a term. Expect:

- 4.5 hours of scheduled contact in a regular week (1.5 h lecture and
  seminar, 3 h studio); 6 hours in each simulation week.
- About 5 – 6 hours of preparation and team work outside class each week:
  readings and videos before the lecture, discipline analysis between CDF
  studios, and procedure study before the simulations.
- Heavier weeks before the two reviews (Weeks 2 and 7) and before each
  simulation (Weeks 10 and 11). Plan for them.

Team work between studios matters: in the CDF weeks your discipline's
numbers are an input to everyone else's.
